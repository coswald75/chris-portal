The Nations in the New Testament

Isaiah's map runs one direction: the nations stream · in

The gathering completes

Follow the series this far and the story completes itself: the studies mapped the nations and showed they were family; this chart shows the family being gathered. The gathering finishes in Revelation — and John describes the finish in Isaiah's own words:

"The nations will walk by its light, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it, and its gates will never be shut by day…" Revelation 21:24–25 — drawing directly on Isaiah 60:3, 11: "Nations shall come to your light, and kings to the brightness of your rising… your gates shall be open continually."

Around the throne stands "a great multitude that no one could number, from every nation, from all tribes and peoples and languages" (Rev 7:9) — the Table of Nations, complete. Ephraim and Judah, Kedar's herdsmen and Tarshish's sailors, a Cushite eunuch with a name better than sons, Egyptians called "my people" — every dial on these charts, pointed home.

And note what remains unfinished. Isaiah 66:19 sends witnesses to peoples "that have not heard my fame" — a verse still in force. The chart ends where the church's assignment begins.

One question is left standing, and it gets its own page next: if the nations are gathered — if the triangles all reorient to one Center — what kind of nation is the church? A people with no tile on the map, no army, no treaty-marriages, and no gods in the trousseau. Isaiah saw that too.
