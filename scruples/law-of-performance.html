Law of Performance

ArticleTalkReadEditNotesHistory
HubThe mechanism. What every stoicheia is running underneath.
The Law of Performance
Working name, under revision
Claim
Every pre-Christian system, religious or not, assigns worth on the basis of performance.
Proposed replacement
“An appearance requires an audience.”
Textual anchor
Col 2:23 — an appearance of wisdom, no value against the flesh.
Failure mode
The system cannot deliver what it advertises, and never says so.

The Law of Performance is the shared mechanism underneath the stoicheia. Systems that look nothing alike — a sacrificial calendar, a philosophical school, a market, a comment section — run the same instruction: perform, and be counted worthy; fail, and be counted unworthy. The content of the performance varies wildly. The structure does not.

The better phrasing

“Law of Performance” implies an audience and never names one, which is a weakness, because the audience is the whole engine. The sharper form, taken from Col 2:23, is that an appearance requires an audience. Ascetic regulations have an appearance of wisdom — appearance to whom? Somebody is watching, or the performance would stop.

Restated this way, every stoicheia is an audience demanding a performance:

SystemThe audienceThe performanceThe promised verdict
KarmaAn impersonal ledgerAccumulated meritA better next round
Honour and shameThe village, the familyFace maintainedBelonging

IdeologyThe movementPurity of positionBeing one of the good
Wellness cultureThe internet; other parentsPurity of intakeSafety, control
Ceremonial observanceRival teachers (Col 2:16)Diet, calendar, ascesisQualification

The last column is the fraud. Col 2:23 is blunt about it: whatever these systems look like, they have no value against the flesh. Paul's diagnosis of the person crushed by them is not that they are trying too hard. It is that the system they are serving cannot deliver what it advertises.

The audience answered

This is where the mechanism hands the argument to Christus Victor in a form sharper than force. Thriambeuō in Col 2:15 is the Roman triumph: a procession, in public, with a watching crowd. Christ put the powers to open shame. The audience you were performing for was marched through the streets as the spectacle. The watchers became the watched.

That is the answer to the audience question in a single image, and it is Paul's, not an import.

The cruel corollary

If worth tracks performance, then suffering must track failure. This inference is not incidental to the performance regime; it is the regime read backwards, and it is everywhere. The disciples ask whose sin caused the blindness (John 9:2). The crowd assumes the Galileans died because they were worse offenders (Luke 13:1–5). The parent assumes every outcome traces to a rule they broke. Jesus refuses the inference in both Gospel cases without softening the call to repent, which is the model for how the refusal has to be preached.

See Suffering as verdict for the fuller treatment, and Scrupulosity for the same algorithm turned inward.

Naming note. If the phrasing changes to “an appearance requires an audience,” this page keeps its title and gains a redirect. The old name is in the preached record from Advent 2025 and a congregation that already learned it should not be made to learn a second one.
