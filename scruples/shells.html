Scaffolding and permanent structures

ArticleTalkReadEditNotesHistory
HubThe distinction that keeps the crab from proving too much.

Not every structure stands in the same relation to Christ's work. Some were scaffolding: given by God, temporary by design, and taken down when the building they served was finished. Others are permanent: still necessary this side of the resurrection, and therefore still contested. Confusing the two produces either legalism or a shrug.

ScaffoldingPermanent
Examples The ceremonial law; sacrifice; calendar; dietary boundary markers; the temple system Marriage; work; government; money; theology; church order; justice; conscience
OriginGod-givenGod-given
Status now Fulfilled and therefore obsolete. The substance has arrived (Col 2:17) Still standing. Still required
Who can occupy it Only an antichrist spirit. Christ has filled it so completely there is nothing left to inhabit Christ — or something else. It will not stay empty
Right response Refuse to go back. Anyone pulling you in is not offering more Christ Fill it. Vacancy is the danger, not the structure
Failure mode Judaising; nostalgia; rebuilt boundary markers made of preference Marriage, work or ideology running on autopilot under a tenant

The test

The question is not “is this structure good?” — both columns are good. It is: has the thing this pointed to arrived? Col 2:17 gives the vocabulary. A shadow is not a lie. It is a real shape cast by a real body. When the body is present, insisting on the shadow is a preference for less.

Rebuilt scaffolding

The live pastoral case is not that anyone wants to resume animal sacrifice. It is that congregations quietly rebuild scaffolding out of cultural preference — music, schooling, dress, diet, political alignment, the unspoken rules about who counts as serious. Christ abolished the ordinances that created hostility (Eph 2:14–16) and we erect new boundary markers on the same foundation.

That is the argument of From Consumerism to Covenant (Jan 2026), and it is why taste gets its own page rather than a mention.

Watch the trap. Preach this badly and you create a new ranking — the free despising the scrupulous — which is scaffolding again, built by the people who just tore it down. See The anti-scrupulous wall.
