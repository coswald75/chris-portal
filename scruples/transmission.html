Transmission: tradition and worldliness

ArticleTalkReadEditNotesHistory
HubNot a further item in the list of stoicheia — the two channels by which any of them arrive.

Colossians 2:8 stacks three prepositional phrases: according to human tradition, according to the elemental principles of the world, and not according to Christ. Read as a list rather than as rhetorical piling-on, Paul has named two channels and one alternative.

That makes tradition and worldliness structurally different from karma or the market. They are not further systems. They are the two ways a system gets into a person.

Neither channel is bad

Paul is not against tradition as such — he uses the same word approvingly for what he delivered (1 Cor 11:2, 2 Thess 2:15). The qualifier is human. And kosmos is not matter; it is the world-order as a going concern. Both channels are how any content gets transmitted, including true content. The question is always what is riding on them.

This is the same shape as the hermit crab one level up: the channel is a good structure, and the trouble is the passenger.

Where it bites

The practical case: a congregation rebuilding scaffolding out of preference. Tradition supplies the authority (“we have always”), worldliness supplies the content (whatever the surrounding culture currently makes salient), and the compound arrives feeling like faithfulness. Neither channel announces itself. That is what makes an inventory necessary rather than optional.

Possible split. This page may be doing too much. If the material on inherited practice grows, split into Tradition and Worldliness with this as the parent.
