Gal 4:3, 4:9; Col 2:8, 2:20. Compare Heb 5:12 (elementary teaching).

A regulatory regime, personally exploited. Neither bare abstraction nor simply a demon.

From the wiki — the argument in progress.

The stoicheia are the elementary principles by which the world sorts people according to performance. Paul uses the term four times, twice in Galatians and twice in Colossians, and in both letters it does the same job: it names what a person is enslaved to before Christ and what a person is being pulled back toward afterward.

The reading taken across this wiki is that the term is deliberately capacious. It covers the Jewish ceremonial calendar and Greek philosophy and pagan cult, and it covers karma, honour and shame, might-makes-right, the market, and ideology, because all of these run the same operating system: do this, and you will be counted worthy. Paul's argument in Colossians 2 does not work if stoicheia means only the Mosaic shadow, because the Colossian error was a hybrid — festivals and new moons in one breath, asceticism and angel-worship in the next.

The term

In ordinary Greek the word means rudiments: the letters of the alphabet in order, the elements of a compound, the first things you learn. Hebrews 5:12 uses it exactly this way, of the elementary teaching a congregation should have outgrown. That register matters. It is not a word for evil. It is a word for basic — and the argument of both Galatians and Colossians is that returning to the basic, after the substance has arrived, is the failure.

This is also why the King's cubit is the right illustration and not merely a serviceable one. A measuring standard is exactly this register: rudimentary, indispensable, and catastrophic when a drifted rod sets itself up as the standard.

Principles or powers

Whether Paul means impersonal principles or personal spirits is a live dispute, and this wiki does not resolve it so much as decline the alternative.

ReadingHeld byStrengthCost
Principles / regulations Calvin; Ridderbos; Horton Fits the ordinary lexical range; fits Heb 5:12; avoids importing a cosmology Paul does not state Strains v. 15, where the rulers are disarmed and paraded — you cannot parade an abstraction
Personal spirits Much modern scholarship Fits the bracketing of v. 15 and Gal 4:8's “beings that by nature are not gods” Strains the plain sense of vv. 21–22, which are rules, not entities
Regime plus occupant (taken here) — Both halves of the chapter stand; explains why Paul can move from cosmic to dietary without a seam Requires the hermit crab to do real work, not just illustrate

The third position is not a compromise. It is a claim: the principles are real and God-made, and the powers are real and have moved into them. Paul can speak of one and mean both because in the world he is describing they are not separable.

Scope

Each of the following is treated on this wiki as a case of the same thing rather than an analogy to it.

  • The ceremonial law — the only one of these that was God-given, and so the only one that is demolished rather than contested.
  • Greek philosophy, pagan cult, karma, honour and shame, might makes right.
  • The market, ideology, wellness culture, taste.
  • Tradition and worldliness — not further items in the list, but the two channels by which any of the above arrive.
The totalising risk. This frame wants to explain everything, and a wiki in which every page links here is a wiki arguing that everything is stoicheia. That is the same too-clean diagnosis the framework exists to resist, only with better Greek. See What is not stoicheia, which is a node and not a caveat.

What Christ did

The claim throughout is that Christ did not abolish the demand. He met it, and met it to excess, so that the regime's own logic now runs in favour of everyone in him. Born under the law (Gal 4:4) is entry into the shell. Fulfilling all righteousness (Matt 3:15) is the proof of ownership. The cancelling of the record of debt is the withdrawal of the powers' standing to prosecute, which is why v. 14 and v. 15 are one sentence and not two.

The pastoral end of this is the whole payload: the question have I done enough is answered not by lowering the bar but by pointing at someone who cleared it on your behalf and in your name.
