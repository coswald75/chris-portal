The hermit crab

ArticleTalkReadEditNotesHistory
Hub · imageAn illustration that became load-bearing. Handle as doctrine, not decoration.
The hermit crab
Occupancy, not creation
Claim
Spiritual powers cannot make structures. They occupy structures God made.
Biblical warrant
Matt 12:43–45 (the swept, empty house); Mark 5:1–13 (the Gerasene's plea to stay).
Corollary
The shell is good. That is why occupation is dangerous rather than obvious.
Sibling image
The tapeworm — parasite, not predator.

A hermit crab does not grow a shell. It finds one, moves in, and wears it — and from outside, what you see is the shell. The claim here is that this is how spiritual powers relate to created order. They are occupants. Everything they hold, they hold second-hand.

Why the image earns its keep

Three things follow immediately, and each of them is hard to say quickly any other way.

The structure is not the enemy. If a demon made the structure, the answer would be demolition. If a demon merely occupies it, the answer is eviction, and the structure survives. Marriage, work, government, money, theology, church order, systems of justice — these are not compromised by having been occupied. They are reclaimed. Col 1:16 is the ground: all things were created through him and for him, which makes occupancy by anything else a usurpation rather than a natural state.

It explains the appearance of wisdom. The reason occupied systems are persuasive is that they are genuinely well made. A good shell is a good shell. Col 2:23 — an appearance of wisdom — is not a claim that these regulations are stupid. It is a claim that something true has been taken over and is now issuing verdicts on behalf of a tenant.

It matches how the Gospels describe the thing. The Gerasene demons beg not to be sent out of the country; they ask for the pigs. Jesus describes an unclean spirit finding a house swept and put in order, and returning with company. Neither passage describes creation. Both describe tenancy — and the second one describes the specific danger of a vacancy.

The empty house

Matthew 12 is the sharp end. Cleaning is not enough. A structure emptied and not filled will be reoccupied, and worse. Applied:

  • If Christ does not inhabit a marriage, something will.
  • If Christ does not rule a person's work, another master will.
  • If Christ does not fill a theology, it will be twisted into heresy.
  • If Christ does not animate a church, it becomes a museum or a cult.
  • If Christ does not occupy an ideology, it becomes total.

The list is only useful if the preceding distinction is in place, because it is not true of every structure. Some shells were never meant to be permanently occupied at all. See Scaffolding and permanent structures.

Conscience as a shell

The most productive application, and the newest, is that conscience is a God-made permanent structure. Romans 2:15 says the law-writing faculty never idles; it will write on the heart whether or not God dictated the text. So the conscience is perfectly happy to work with codes God never wrote.

Occupied by Christ, conscience convicts and then releases. Left vacant, an accuser moves in wearing a borrowed code — and the faculty still functions, still issues verdicts, still sounds exactly like conscience. That is scrupulosity described structurally rather than moralistically, and it is why the answer cannot be “have less conscience.”

Do not over-drive this. The image explains occupancy. It does not explain every distress. A sick brain is not a tenant, and a tender conscience is not necessarily an occupied one — on one reading it is a sign of good soil. Applying the crab to those cases hands a tormented person one more thing to fear. See What is not stoicheia.

Relation to the tapeworm

The tapeworm image (preached Oct 2025 on 1 John 2, reused on Colossians in Dec 2025) is the same doctrine in a different register: parasite, not predator; anti as “in replacement of” rather than “against”; the lie always being Jesus plus something. Not everything that moves in is sent to help.

The crab is better for structures. The tapeworm is better for doctrine and for good desires turned into leverage — a lot of dark spiritual things do not deny Christ, they insist on lending him a hand.
