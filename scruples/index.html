Article of the day

The hermit crab is the governing image for how spiritual powers relate to created structures. A hermit crab cannot make a shell. It finds one, moves in, and wears it. The claim is that demons work the same way: they are not creators but occupants, and every structure they hold was made good by God before it was taken.

This does two things at once. It explains why the occupied structure still looks trustworthy — the shell is genuinely well made — and it explains why the answer is eviction rather than demolition. Jesus' teaching about the swept and empty house (Matt 12:43–45) and the Gerasene demoniac's plea not to be sent out of the country (Mark 5:10) both read as accounts of tenancy rather than of creation.

The image also sorts the shells into two kinds, which turns out to be the load-bearing distinction in the whole framework: scaffolding that Christ has already taken down, and permanent structures that will be occupied by somebody.

  • …that tradition and worldliness are not analogies for the stoicheia but Paul's own two named vectors for them, stacked in adjacent clauses in Col 2:8?
  • …that stoicheia in ordinary Greek meant something closer to the ABCs — a row of letters, a basic series — which is why the King's cubit is a better illustration than it first appears?

About this wiki

This is a working file, not a finished position. It exists to hold one argument — that Paul's stoicheia tou kosmou names a universal performance regime, that spiritual powers occupy God-made structures rather than building their own, and that Christ's victory was juridical before it was forceful — and to show where that argument touches everything else.

Pages are short on purpose. The value is in the links, not the essays. Every claim that has a home elsewhere should be a link to that home rather than a paragraph repeated here.

How this is built

Ordinary wikis have one kind of link. This one has seven, because the whole point is to show how two ideas touch, not just that they do. Every link in an article body carries a type:

  • instantiates — a particular case of a general thing
  • inhabits — occupies a structure it did not make
  • fulfils — satisfies and so ends a demand
  • guards against — limits or corrects another page
  • illustrates — carries an idea in an image
  • disputes — holds a genuinely rival reading
  • transmits — how the thing arrives in a person

The rule that keeps this honest: a page may not link to Stoicheia merely because it is nearby. If the relation cannot be typed, it is not a link.

Text of the day

Colossians 2:8 sets three prepositional phrases side by side: according to human tradition, according to the elemental principles of the world, and not according to Christ. Read as a list rather than as a rhetorical pile, it names the two channels and the one alternative.
