Colossians 2:8–15

Article Talk Read Edit Notes History
Scripture The governing text. Most of this wiki is a commentary on eight verses.
Colossians 2:8–15
The pericope this wiki hangs on
Structure
Warning (8) → fullness (9–10) → union (11–13) → cancellation and triumph (14–15).
Key terms
stoicheia, cheirographon, thriambeuō.
Preached
Dec 2025, Demonic Tapeworms.

v. 8 — the three phrases

The warning is against being taken captive by philosophy and empty deceit, and then three prepositional phrases: according to human tradition, according to the elemental principles of the world, and not according to Christ. Two channels and an alternative. See Transmission.

Note what is not said: philosophy is not the problem as such. What is measured is which standard a thing runs by.

vv. 9–13 — fullness and union

The whole fullness of deity dwells bodily in Christ, and — the move that matters — you have been filled in him. The vocabulary of occupancy is already Paul's before any illustration is added to it. Then burial and raising in baptism (vv. 11–13), which is what v. 20's “you died to” refers back to.

vv. 14–15 — one sentence

The record of debt with its legal demands is set aside, nailed to the cross — and by that act the rulers and authorities are disarmed and put to open shame, paraded in triumph. The two halves are not two topics. The disarming consists in the cancellation. See Christus Victor and Accusation.

Thriambeuō is the Roman triumph procession: public, with a crowd. This is the detail that answers the audience question raised on Law of Performance.

The hinge at v. 16

Paul's own “therefore” runs from cosmic victory straight into food, drink, festival and new moon. That transition is the warrant for treating scrupulosity as native to this text rather than imported into it. Anyone preaching vv. 8–15 without vv. 16–23 has stopped one verse before Paul's application.

In the preached record

Demonic Tapeworms (Dec 2025) works the whole chapter on an alternating skeleton — Parasitic Promise / Christ Is More Than Enough, ten times through, landing on vv. 20–21. It contains the charity paragraph the later scrupulosity material needs: the devil can use good desires, and a lot of dark spiritual things do not deny Christ but insist on lending him a hand.
