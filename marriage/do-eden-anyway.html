Do Eden Anyway

Part of the Crown Each Day wiki.

Definition: The conclusion of the preacher of Ecclesiastes, compressed into a phrase: the fall changed the conditions of human life, but not its content. Sin and death entered the world; vanity settled like dust on everything; work became toil. But what a human being is, and what a human being is for, has not changed. So do the things Eden was made of — work, worship, relationships, celebration — under cursed conditions, by faith. Do Eden anyway.

Where the phrase comes from

The phrase emerged from the 2026 Ecclesiastes series, in the image of a surveyor walking a blast site. The preacher of Ecclesiastes is not a cynic; he is a man inspecting a world after a bomb has gone off — the bomb of Genesis 3 — and finding the layer of dust on everything he examines.

From the Hall — The Earthy Piety of Providence (2026-08-09, Ecclesiastes 9:7–10): "He's surveying a cursed world and he sees the layer of dust on everything. It's like a bomb went off... And I think that the writer, the preacher in Ecclesiastes is surveying all of this fallout and all this dust, and you know what he winds up saying?... Here's the phrase: do Eden anyway. Do Eden anyway. Is it harder? Yeah. The word for work is now toil. It's all harder. It all takes more faith and more patience and more grit. But who we are has not changed as human beings, and the way to live a good life has not changed in spite of all the hardship that surrounds us... Yes, death and despair and uncertainty have entered the world. Yes, there's a layer of vanity on everything, but none of that changes what you are and what you are for. Do Eden anyway. Do your work. Walk with your God. Love the people God put in your life. Crown each day with the celebration of the fruit of your labor."

The full inventory

Ecclesiastes explores every venue of human pursuit and finds vanity on all of it — uncertainty, unfairness, thorns everywhere. And yet the preacher keeps returning, some ten times, to a short list of things that remain good for a human to do. In Ecclesiastes 9:7–10 the whole inventory appears in one place:

Go, eat your bread with joy, and drink your wine with a merry heart, for God has already approved what you do. Let your garments be always white. Let not oil be lacking on your head. Enjoy life with the wife whom you love, all the days of your vain life that he has given you under the sun... Whatever your hand finds to do, do it with your might. (Ecclesiastes 9:7–10)

Bread and wine — celebration. Garments and oil — ordinary care of the body. The wife you love — relationships. Whatever your hand finds to do — work. The series summarized the categories memorably:

From the Hall — Do Eden Anyway: Work (2026-08-16, Ecclesiastes 10:9–12:14): "After a thorough investigation into the nature of life, the preacher... explored every possible venue for human pursuits and found vanity on all of it. Basically, uncertainty, unfairness, thorns everywhere. And yet his conclusion is, there are certain things that still a human must do and that are good for a human to do. And that is summarized in what we said last week: worship, work, wife, and wine."

These are not consolation prizes for a ruined world. They are the original Eden vocations, still standing.

The curse changed the conditions, not the vocation

The exegetical backbone of the phrase is Genesis 3 read carefully: God does not revoke the creation mandate at the fall; he reconfigures it.

From the Hall — Outgrowing Anxiety, Part 4 (2025-12-23): "After the fall, God doesn't fully revoke dominion. He just reconfigures it... Dominion remains, but it's achieved through suffering... Crucially, Adam and Eve never lose their vocation. Their vocation is simply now exercised under cursed conditions."

This is why Ecclesiastes says toil rather than work. Toil is not a neutral synonym; it is a curse-word for work, carrying the whole connotation burden of Genesis 3. Doing Eden anyway means accepting that every Eden vocation now costs more — more faith, more patience, more grit — and doing it at the higher price.

The Christological ground

Left to itself, "do Eden anyway" is stoicism with a garden theme. The series is explicit that it must not be left to itself:

From the Hall — Narcan for the Soul (2026-06-28, Ecclesiastes 2:13–3:22): "You're told ten times to enjoy your toil and enjoy the fruits of your toil... But friends, I want to be super clear about this. The uniquely Christian approach starts by enjoying the fruit of his toil... Day one of your life as a Christian is not you enjoying the fruit of your work. Your life as a Christian is built on the fruit of his work. Specifically, his toil."

The last Adam has come, restored the original dominion, and put the world under new management. The Christian does Eden anyway not as a coping strategy but as a citizen of a world already being reclaimed — enjoying the fruit of Christ's toil first, and only then the fruit of his own.

In this wiki

The practical form this takes at the household level is the subject of the rest of the wiki: Crown Each Day names the daily unit of doing Eden anyway, and The Evening Routine is the family game plan for actually doing it.
