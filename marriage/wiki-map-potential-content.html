Wiki Map — Potential Content

Part of the Crown Each Day wiki. This is the working map: what's built, what's promised, and what's on the horizon. Pages move up the list as they're written.

Built

  • Crown Each Day — the hub and namesake: ending each day deliberately, with celebration and shared enjoyment of the fruit of labor. The phrase's genealogy (Psalm 65:11, finis coronat opus, Compline) and the muzzle-versus-celebration vice pair.
  • Do Eden Anyway — the series thesis: the fall changed the conditions of human life, not its content. Worship, work, wife, and wine, under cursed conditions, built on the fruit of Christ's toil.
  • The Evening Routine — the family game plan that crowns each day. The Hebrew day begins at evening; the routine faces both directions; nail your evenings.
  • Home as Walled Garden — the home as safe, ordered ground for the cultivation of your most essential work. The Augustinian heat map, clutter as postponed decisions, walls and a gate, slán abhaile.
  • Slán Abhaile — the blessing turned audit: is your little Eden safe? Family systems theory weighed and used; safe from the great Diabolos.
  • Ordinary Transmutation — God gives grass and grapes; wine, oil, and bread come through tended process. Alchemy has been around forever; the real gold is made at home.
  • Wife — The Garden Within the Garden — Ecclesiastes 9:9's one-verse wife, the canon's whole-book answer, cultivation and guarding, the little foxes.
  • Song of Solomon — Eden's song sung in Jerusalem; the mechanics of the genre with Fireflies for Angela as the household demonstration; how you tend a garden-wife (with words), and what kind.
  • The Ballad of the Two Kings — Alfred and Oswald in ballad form: the wall and the door. (Draft — lyrics complete, may yet be set to music.)
  • King Oswald — the open hand. How the Irish saved civilization, hearth to hearth: Patrick, the monasteries, Iona; the exiled prince baptized at an Irish fire; Heavenfield, Aidan and the milk of gentler teaching, the translator-king; the Easter silver dish and the hand that never perished. Basic generosity flows out of a kept hearth.

Promised (links already installed, pages not yet written)

  • Hospitality — the gate in the garden wall. Foreshadowed on Home as Walled Garden: not everyone is allowed in, and no one is allowed in all the time — the wall is what makes welcome possible, because only a kept garden has something to open. King Oswald's silver dish is the patron story. The relationship between guarding and giving; the table extended without the garden being trampled.
  • The Evening Routine — the game plan build-out — the "shape of the routine" section is standing open with raw materials staged: the shared meal, spoken gratitude, the people at the table, the forward-facing close. Awaiting the full family game plan.

Planned

  • King Alfred — the hearth-king. Burned the cakes and learned the lesson: a throne is kept by keeping watch on ovens, walls, and wheat. After Edington, the quieter war: burhs (walls), the law code (order), translated psalms (the word in the house), and the candle-clock — six marked candles portioning every day between work, rest, and prayer. Alfred as the historical patron of crowning each day: a king who measured his days on purpose and rebuilt a burned world — Eden done anyway, at kingdom scale.

Horizon (candidates, in no particular order)

  • Psalm 128 — the wiki in one psalm — "Your wife will be like a fruitful vine within your house; your children will be like olive shoots around your table." Vine, house, table, and the fruit of your labor eaten with blessing — every page of this wiki compressed into four verses.
  • Children — olive shoots around the table — the second crop of the garden within the garden; what the evening routine does to a childhood, compounded over eighteen years of evenings.
  • The Table — feasting as the load-bearing act of celebration: bread and wine from Ecclesiastes 9 to the Passover's four cups to the Lord's Supper — the true feast the daily table rehearses.
  • Sabbath — the weekly crown, instituted before the fall: rest ordained not because work became hard but because we are finite. The day-scale crown (evening) and the week-scale crown (sabbath) as one system.
  • Friendship — Ecclesiastes 4's two-are-better-than-one and the Lewis principle: nobody has fully enjoyed anything until someone shares it. The guest list of the crowned day.
  • Morning — the other bookend: orientation before the day tests you (creature–creation, arrow prayers) — the morning routine as the evening routine's complement, not its competitor.
