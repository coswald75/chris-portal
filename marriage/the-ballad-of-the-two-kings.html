The Ballad of the Two Kings

Part of the Crown Each Day wiki — a ballad bridging the wiki's two movements: Alfred the hearth-king who guarded the wall, and Oswald the open-handed who kept the gate — the door through which Hospitality will enter this wiki. (Draft.)

The Danes came down like winter wolves
and ate the summer land;
they burned the barns of Wessex
from the Severn to the sand.
The king fled to the marshes
with no crown upon his head,
to mind a poor wife's fire —
and he burned her daily bread.

She scolded him, no king to her,
who let the loaves go black,
and he took it like a man who'd learned
what kings must not let slack:
a throne is kept by keeping watch
on ovens, walls, and wheat —
the man who cannot tend a hearth
will lose on every street.

So mark the tallow candle,
and set the table well,
for every day's a kingdom,
as Alfred's cakes could tell —
the Danes are always coming,
the fire's always low,
so crown the day you're given
and do Eden as you go.

Then out of mist at Egbert's Stone
the shire-men swelled his ranks,
and Guthrum's host at Edington
broke backward to their banks;
and home he went and built the walls
and wrote the judgments down,
he marked his candles into hours
and wore his working crown.

Now rewind two hundred winters
to a harder, wilder north,
where an exile out of Iona
brought the cross of Jesus forth:
at Heavenfield he raised it,
rough timber in the sod,
and the king who knelt beside it
won the morning with his God.

He set a see at Lindisfarne,
gave Aidan leave to teach,
and stood interpreter himself
to put Christ in English speech;
one Eastertide at table,
with the silver piled high,
came word of hungry strangers
at the gate, about to die.

He sent them out the royal feast,
then broke the dish as well,
and Aidan caught his giving hand:
"May this hand never fail."
They cut him down at Maserfield,
they cut the king apart —
but the hand that fed the hungry
never crumbled into dark.

So mark the tallow candle,
and set the table well,
for every day's a kingdom,
as Alfred's cakes could tell —
the Danes are always coming,
the fire's always low,
so crown the day you're given
and do Eden as you go.

So here's to hearth-king Alfred,
who learned to watch the bread,
and here's to open-handed Oswald,
who kept the strangers fed;
one guarded, one gave freely —
the wall and then the door —
and if your name is Oswald,
you know what your name is for.

Historical notes

Alfred: the Athelney marshes and the burned-cakes legend, the muster at Egbert's Stone, the victory over Guthrum at Edington (878), the burh system of fortified towns, the law code, his own translations of the psalms and Boethius, and the six marked candle-clocks by which he portioned each day between work, rest, and prayer. Oswald (per Bede): exile and conversion on Iona, the wooden cross raised at Heavenfield before the battle with Cadwallon (634), serving personally as Bishop Aidan's interpreter at the Northumbrian court, the Easter feast at which he sent the meal and the broken-up silver dish out to the poor at the gate, Aidan's blessing — "may this hand never perish" — and the hand kept incorrupt at Bamburgh after his death at Maserfield (642). The name Oswald: os, god + weald, power, rule.
