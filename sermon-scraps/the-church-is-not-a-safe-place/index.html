Preaching Outline · Revelation 2–3

The Church Is Not a Safe Place

Why the church is unsafe — and why that is the best news it has.

Big idea

This is a thematic survey of the first, second, and third chapters of the book of Revelation. The main idea being that the church is not a safe place, because it is the place where the living Christ actually is. It is supernatural, contested, watched, and judged — and that is the best news the church has, because the one who makes it unsafe is the one who loves it. Not safe. Good.

Point 1 — It Is a Supernatural Place

The claimStepping onto the visible edge of an invisible order

When you walk into church you are not joining a civic organization that happens to mention God. You are stepping onto the visible edge of an invisible order.

Movement 1The cosmic hierarchy of 1:1–5 — the church is the terminus of a transmission from the throne

Walk the chain slowly; let the room feel the altitude:

God → Jesus Christ → his angel → John → (the angels of the churches) → the churches → “his servants.”

  • Ἀποκάλυψις Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ ἣν ἔδωκεν αὐτῷ ὁ θεός (1:1) — the Father gives it to the Son. ἐσήμανεν ἀποστείλας διὰ τοῦ ἀγγέλου αὐτοῦ τῷ δούλῳ αὐτοῦ Ἰωάννῃ — through the angel, to the servant John. Then “John to the seven churches” (1:4).
  • Preaching line: this book traveled farther to reach you than any letter ever mailed. It crossed the distance between the throne of God and a Sunday morning. The church sits at the receiving end of a courier route that begins in the Godhead and passes through angelic hands.
  • The chain re-appears as liturgy in ch. 5: the Father hands the scroll to the Lamb and three widening rings of heaven erupt — elders, myriads of angels, every creature. What the church receives quietly on the Lord’s Day, heaven celebrates at volume.

Movement 2The hierarchy has a downlink into every congregation — angels and lampstands

  • 1:16, 20 — seven stars in Christ’s right hand = “the angels of the seven churches”; seven lampstands = the churches, and the Son of Man walks among them (1:13; 2:1). Every letter is addressed “to the angel of the church in…” Whatever position one takes on the identity of the angels (heavenly representatives vs. human leaders — flag the debate, don’t die on it), the address itself makes the point: each ordinary congregation has a presence in the heavenly order. Ephesus has an address in the unseen world.
  • Cross-references that say the same thing without symbols:
    • Eph 3:10 — God’s manifold wisdom is being made known “through the church to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places.” The church is not watching the cosmic drama; the church is the exhibit. The powers learn about God by watching us.
    • Heb 12:22–24 — “you have come to Mount Zion… to innumerable angels in festal gathering, to the assembly of the firstborn… to Jesus.” Present tense. On Sunday morning you are not near that gathering; you have come to it.
    • 1 Pet 1:12 — “things into which angels long to look.” 1 Cor 11:10 — “because of the angels.” The apostles assume an angelic audience in the room.

Movement 3A supernatural place is a contested place — the warfare texts

  • Eph 6:12 — “we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness, against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places.” Note where Paul puts this: at the end of Ephesians — a letter to the first church on Revelation’s mailing list. The armor passage is addressed to a congregation, plural verbs, a church suiting up together.
  • Col 1:16; 2:15 — thrones, dominions, rulers, authorities created through him and for him; and at the cross he “disarmed the rulers and authorities and put them to open shame, triumphing over them.” The powers are real, and they are defeated — both truths at full strength.
  • Revelation agrees in narrative form: the dragon, furious with the woman, goes off “to make war on the rest of her offspring… those who keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus” (12:17). The church is the dragon’s remaining target. No wonder it isn’t safe.

The landing of Point 1Someone is there

If the church were merely human, it would be as safe as a book club. It is unsafe for the same reason the summit of Sinai was unsafe: someone is there. The lampstands are not decorative — the Son of Man is walking among them tonight. (This hands off directly to Point 2: a place this significant draws enemies — and to Point 3: a Presence this near sees everything.)

Corpus callback — “Asaph’s Odyssey” (2025-07-13, Ps 73) already laid this foundation for the congregation — the church as God’s appointed place vs. the “I can worship on the golf course” objection: “when you walk into a place that has been appointed by God… you’re plugging into something, a story, and it’s much bigger than you.” The room has the category; Point 1 escalates it from appointed place to supernatural address.

Point 2 — It Is a Struggling Place (sketch — to develop)

Enemies within and without — and the surprise is how many are within. Tally the seven letters: only Smyrna and Philadelphia are not rebuked, and their afflictions are external (prison, slander, “synagogue of Satan”). The other five bleed from inside: self-styled apostles (2:2), the Nicolaitans present in two churches (2:6, 15), the teaching of Balaam (2:14), Jezebel tolerated in Thyatira (2:20 — the indictment is not her presence but the church’s tolerance), Sardis dead under a live reputation (3:1), Laodicea blind to itself (3:17). In the era famous for pagan persecution, five of seven diagnoses are internal.

  • Killer cross-reference: Acts 20:29–30 — Paul, to the Ephesian elders: wolves will come in among you, “and from among your own selves will arise men speaking twisted things.” Rev 2:2 is that prophecy, forty years on, half-fulfilled and half-resisted — Ephesus tested and exposed the false apostles. The warning worked.
  • Without: Antipas killed where Satan’s throne is (2:13); tribulation, poverty, prison, “faithful unto death” (2:9–10); the coming hour of trial (3:10).
  • Supporting: Jude 4 (“crept in unnoticed”), 1 John 2:19 (“went out from us”), Matt 13 (weeds among wheat).
  • Spurgeon — the reframe that turns the struggle into evidence (Metropolitan Tabernacle Pulpit, vol. 37 [1891]):

    If the devil never roars, the Church will never sing! God is not doing much if the devil is not awake and busy. Depend upon it: a working Christ makes a raging devil! When you hear ill reports, cruel speeches, threats, taunts and the like, believe that the Lord is among His people and is working gloriously.

    This is the pivot of Point 2: the struggle is not a sign the church is failing — it is a sign the church is the church. A raging devil is downstream of a working Christ. Smyrna and Philadelphia, the two unrebuked churches, are also the two under the heaviest fire.
  • To decide: historical and contemporary examples for “within.”

Point 3 — It Is a Seen Place

Οἶδα — “I know” — opens all seven letters (2:2, 9, 13, 19; 3:1, 8, 15). Eyes “like a flame of fire” (1:14; repeated to Thyatira, 2:18). The climax: 2:23 — “I am he who searches mind and heart, and I will give to each of you according to your works” — Jesus claiming Jeremiah 17:10, a divine prerogative, in first person. Heb 4:13: “no creature is hidden… all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account.” He is “the firstborn of the dead” (1:5) and the appointed “judge of the living and the dead” (Acts 10:42; 1 Pet 4:5).

  • The gaze cuts both ways and the sermon must say so: the same “I know” that exposes Sardis comforts Smyrna — “I know your tribulation and your poverty (but you are rich)” (2:9). Being fully seen is terror or consolation depending on what there is to see — and for the one covered in Christ, it ends as consolation.

Point 4 — It Is a Sifting Place

There are consequences, and they are administered by Jesus, to churches. The catalogue is startling when read in one breath: I will remove your lampstand (2:5); I will war against them with the sword of my mouth (2:16); I will throw her onto a sickbed… and strike her children dead, and all the churches will know (2:22–23); I will come like a thief (3:3); I will spit you out of my mouth (3:16). Nobody in Asia Minor needed Rome to make church dangerous; the danger was already standing among the lampstands.

  • The governor that keeps this from becoming terror: 3:19 — “Those whom I love, I reprove and discipline.” Same voice, same verse-breath as the threats. Cf. Heb 12:5–11; 1 Cor 11:30–32; 1 Pet 4:17 — “judgment begins at the household of God.”
  • The sobering historical footnote: the lampstands were removed. The churches of Asia Minor are ruins you can visit. The threat of 2:5 was not rhetorical.

Conclusion — Not safe. Good.

The four points reverse into gospel. It is supernatural — so the Presence that makes it dangerous is the Presence that makes it matter. It is struggling — so no one should leave a church for being a battlefield; that is the basic nature of the church. It is seen — and the eyes of fire belong to the one who “loves us and has freed us from our sins by his blood” (1:5). It is sanctioned — and the discipline is the love (3:19).

Aslan — this is where he lands, once, at full weight:

“Safe? … Who said anything about safe? ’Course he isn’t safe. But he’s good. He’s the King, I tell you.”

— Mr. Beaver, The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe

The church is not a safe place — but it is a good place, because the one who makes it unsafe is good. Spurgeon says the rest (from “The Best Donation,” MTP no. 2234 [1891], on 2 Cor 8:5):

If I had never joined a Church till I had found one that was perfect, I should never have joined one at all! And the moment I did join it, if I had found one, I should have spoiled it, for it would not have been perfect after I became a member of it. Still, imperfect as it is, it is the dearest place on earth to us.

Not safe, not perfect — and still the dearest place on earth. Then the sweep: to the one who conquers — seven promises, one inheritance refracted: the tree, the crown, the name, the throne — every facet of the New Jerusalem, distributed to the churches wound by wound, all of it already his (“as I also conquered,” 3:21), all of it given. The hand that holds the seven stars is the hand that was laid on John: “Fear not.” (1:17). The church is not a safe place. It is something far better: it is the place where he walks.

Footnote for the manuscript: both Spurgeon quotes date from 1891 — the last full year of his ministry, preached from inside the Downgrade Controversy that broke his health. The man commending the imperfect church as the dearest place on earth, and reading the devil’s roar as evidence of Christ’s work, was bleeding from church wounds when he said it.
