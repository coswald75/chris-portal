When Silence is Sin

Discerning When We Should Speak Up and “Say the Thing”

A self-examination worksheet on speaking the truth in love — for those moments when the fear of another person's pain or reaction tempts us to withhold what should be said. Write honestly and specifically; there is no score and no grade.

Before you begin

Edwin Friedman, in his family-systems thinking (A Failure of Nerve), observed that people often withhold truth or avoid necessary confrontation not primarily because the other person “couldn't handle it,” but because the withholder has a low tolerance for the other person's emotional pain, distress, or reactive response. This leads to secrecy, enabling, triangulation, and emotional fusion that ultimately harms the relationship and prevents growth. The secret-keeper prioritizes short-term relational “peace” — or their own comfort — over long-term health.

In biblical categories, this maps powerfully onto truth and love, walking in the light, integrity vs. deception, and the fear of man vs. the fear of God:

Speaking the truth in love (Ephesians 4:15) — Biblical love is not sentimental avoidance of discomfort. It is courageous, others-oriented honesty that builds up the body of Christ toward maturity. Withholding truth out of a low “pain tolerance” is often a failure to love well.

Walking in the light (1 John 1:5-7) — God is light, and in Him is no darkness. Fellowship and cleansing happen in the light; hiding truth to spare someone (or oneself) pain is walking in darkness.

Integrity and the fear of the Lord (Proverbs 12:22; 29:25) — “The fear of man lays a snare.” Rationalizing secrecy as “protecting” someone is often idolatry of human approval over obedience to God.

Confrontation and restoration (Matthew 18:15-17; Galatians 6:1-2) — Direct, private confrontation first, not avoidance; restoring the sinning brother gently while bearing burdens, entering the pain rather than shielding from it.

Maturity through suffering (Romans 5:3-5; James 1:2-4) — Trials and facing consequences produce perseverance, character, and hope. Shielding people chronically from truth stunts this.

In short, the secret-keeper's low pain tolerance is a form of selfish fear masquerading as love. Biblical love and truth-telling require dying to self, trusting God with the outcomes, and believing the Spirit can use truth to bring repentance, healing, and deeper fellowship.

Purpose & Instructions This worksheet is meant to help you examine ways you may be withholding truth due to a low tolerance for others' pain (or your own discomfort), repent, and practice gospel-motivated honesty that builds biblical love and fellowship. Work through it daily or as your counselor specifies. Be specific and concrete. Everything you write is saved on this device; when you're ready you can print it or share a finished copy with Pastor Chris.
1. Scripture Study and Meditation

Read and meditate on the following passages (15–20 minutes daily). For each one, answer in your own words: What does this teach about truth, honesty, love, or walking in the light? How does it expose any “pain tolerance” issues in my life?

Ephesians 4:15, 25-32 — Speak the truth in love; put away falsehood

1 John 1:5-10 — Walk in the light; confession and cleansing

Proverbs 27:5-6 & 29:25 — “Faithful are the wounds of a friend”; the fear of man

Galatians 6:1-2 & Matthew 18:15-17 — Restoring others

Colossians 3:9-10 & Ephesians 4:22-24 — Put off the old self; speak truth

Memorize this weekEphesians 4:15 and 1 John 1:7.
2. Self-Examination

Prayerfully answer these questions. Complete once, then review daily.

In what current relationship(s) am I tempted to keep a secret, withhold important information, or avoid a hard conversation because I fear the other person's pain, anger, disappointment, or rejection? Be specific (e.g., a family secret, a financial issue, an observed sin).

What am I telling myself to rationalize this? (“It would destroy them”; “They couldn't handle it”; “It's kinder this way”; “I'll lose access to them or their kids.”) How does this compare to Scripture? With this approach, am I trusting myself or God?

How is this rooted in my own fear, selfishness, or desire for comfort rather than love for God and neighbor?

What potential good (repentance, growth, deeper trust) might come if truth is brought into the light? What risks am I trusting God with?

3. Repentance and Prayer

Pray through these daily.

Confess specific instances of deceit or avoidance to God. Ask for forgiveness through Christ's blood (1 John 1:9).

Pray for courage, wisdom, and a higher “pain threshold” to love others biblically. Write your prayer. (e.g., “Lord, help me walk in the light as You are in the light. Give me grace to speak truth in love, trusting You with the results.”)

Pray for the person(s) involved — for their heart to receive truth, and for your relationship to reflect gospel fellowship.

4. Practical Application Steps

Choose one situation to address this week (start small if needed; you can build). Plan it and carry it out.

Prepare

Write out, in your own words, what truth needs to be spoken, how it connects to Scripture, and how you will aim for restoration and love (tone, timing, setting).

Do it

Have the conversation privately and gently. Focus on facts, your own heart, and the other's good. Note what you will do — or what happened when you did.

Follow up

Journal what happened, how you handled their reaction (or the anticipated pain), and what God taught you. If appropriate, involve a wise third party for accountability.

A word of cautionIf the situation feels too complex or dangerous, discuss alternatives with Pastor Chris or church leadership first (e.g., involving church leadership per Matthew 18).
5. Accountability and Reflection

Who is a trusted fellow believer you will share this assignment with for prayer and accountability this week?

Report back to them on your progress. Notes on those conversations:

At the end of the week, write a one-page summary: What did I learn about my heart? How has walking in the light changed my view of love? What will I continue doing?

Expected outcome Greater freedom in Christ, deeper relationships marked by truth and love, and growth in Christlike courage. This assignment puts feet on the theology: truth in love isn't optional; it's the path to real fellowship and sanctification. If you're struggling, remember the cross — Jesus endured ultimate pain to bring us into the light. He gives grace to follow Him.
