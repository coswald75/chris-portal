A word from Chris

This is a commonplace book — the old practice of keeping your reading and thinking under interlinked headings, loci, so that any one of them opens onto the rest. Protestants have done theology this way since Melanchthon, and printed the chart before the text since Perkins.

The six fields on the map are not departments. They are constructed from one another: same angles, same operation, each derived from the field before it. The household runs into the city, the city into first things, first things back into how a man speaks to his wife on a Tuesday night. The drawing is the argument.

The tooling is the only new part. Over the past year I’ve used large language models to index and cross-reference a long archive of sermons and study notes, surfacing connections I’d made without noticing them. The judgments are mine, and they are provisional. Argue with them — that’s what this is for.

A locus in Chris’s commonplace book — one heading that opens onto the rest.
