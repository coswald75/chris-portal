Riv (רִיב) — The Cause Taken Up: Advocacy and Prayer

Part of Litigating Without a License. Riv is the lawsuit word — and Scripture's great surprise is who keeps taking up whose. God takes up ours; we are commanded to take up the widow's; and prayer is the courtroom where a believer takes up his brother's. Intercession is the licensed form of having a case about someone. You cannot occupy the prosecution and defense tables at the same time.

The word

Riv (verb רִיב, noun רִיב): to contend, dispute at law, plead a cause; a lawsuit, a legal claim. Paired with Madon at Jer 15:10 and Hab 1:3. Like Din, it is a two-edged word:

Unlicensed — the quarrel: - Prov 17:1 — better a dry morsel with quiet than a house full of feasting with riv - Prov 20:3 — "it is an honor for a man to keep aloof from riv, but every fool will be quarreling" - Prov 17:14 — quit before the riv breaks out; Prov 3:30 — do not contend without cause - Prov 25:8–10 — the procedural gem: "do not hastily bring into court... argue your case (riv) with your neighbor himself, and do not reveal another's secret, lest he who hears you bring shame upon you." Take the riv to the party, directly, confidentially — the same licensed process as Lev 19:17 and Matt 18:15, and the exact opposite of the whisper network - Prov 26:17 — meddling in a riv not your own = grabbing a passing dog by the ears

Licensed — the cause taken up: - Prov 22:23 — "the LORD will plead their cause (yariv rivam) and rob of life those who rob them" (the poor at the gate) - Prov 23:11 — "their Redeemer (go'el) is strong; he will plead their cause against you" — note the office: the go'el is the kinsman-advocate, a legal role. Redemption is courtroom work - Jer 50:34 — "their Redeemer is strong... he will surely plead their cause" - Ps 35:1 — "Contend, O LORD, with those who contend with me"; Ps 43:1 — "plead my cause (riva rivi)"; Lam 3:58 — "You have taken up my cause, O Lord; you have redeemed my life" - Mic 7:9 — the summit: "I will bear the indignation of the LORD, because I have sinned against him, until he pleads my cause and executes judgment for me." The offended Judge becomes the offender's Advocate. This is the gospel in riv form - 1 Sam 24:15; 25:39 — David twice hands over a riv he could have executed himself: sparing Saul in the cave ("may the LORD plead my cause and deliver me from your hand"), and after Abigail stops his raid ("Blessed be the LORD who has pleaded the cause of my reproach from the hand of Nabal"). The pattern: surrender the case or it becomes madon — "never avenge yourselves, but leave it to the wrath of God" (Rom 12:19) is riv-transfer - Isa 1:17 — and it is commanded to us, downhill: "bring justice to the fatherless, plead the widow's cause" — followed immediately (1:18) by God's own astonishing summons, "Come now, let us reason together... though your sins are like scarlet" — the covenant lawsuit that ends in cleansing rather than sentence (cf. God's riv against his people: Mic 6:1–2; Hos 4:1; Jer 2:9 — even his prosecution woos)

Prayer as the believer's riv

Here is the page's center. When you see something in your brother, your husband, your neighbor, your enemy — real data, maybe real sin — you are going to take up a case about them somewhere. There are only two venues: the whisper network and the silent tribunal (unlicensed), or the bench itself (licensed). Intercession is filing the case with the only Judge who has jurisdiction — as counsel for the defense.

The two continuous activities in Scripture make the choice stark: the accuser accuses "day and night" (Rev 12:10); Christ "always lives to make intercession" (Heb 7:25). Note that Rom 8:34 — the closed-docket text — does not end with the cross: "Who is to condemn? Christ Jesus is the one who died... who indeed is interceding for us." Advocacy is the present tense of the cross. Every believer's speech about other people joins one of the two ongoing practices. Being more of an advocate than an accuser is, concretely, being more of a pray-er than a talker.

Praying for your brother: - 1 John 5:16 — "If anyone sees his brother committing a sin... he shall ask, and God will give him life." Note the epistemology: John assumes you will see real data. The commanded first response to the sight of a brother's sin is not a verdict but a petition - James 5:16 — "confess your sins to one another and pray for one another, that you may be healed." The inversion that defines gossip: gossip is confessing someone else's sins to a third party. Intercession carries the same information to the Advocate instead - Job 42:8–10 — "my servant Job shall pray for you"... "the LORD restored the fortunes of Job when he had prayed for his friends." The accused becomes advocate for his accusers — and his own restoration rides on the intercession - Luke 22:31–32 — the two filings, side by side: "Satan demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat — but I have prayed for you, that your faith may not fail." One motion from the prosecution, one from the defense, over the same defendant and the same damning data (Peter will deny him — and Jesus intercedes knowing it)

Praying for your husband (and wife): - 1 Pet 3:1–7 — the whole passage runs on this: the wife's power is "without a word" toward the husband and with words toward God; the husband must honor his wife "so that your prayers may not be hindered" — marriage's health is measured at the bench. The alternative venues are Michal's window (the silent verdict, 2 Sam 6:16) and the whisper to friends; the licensed venue is the closet. Sarah's honorable inner speech (Gen 18:12 ← 1 Pet 3:6) shows the tribunal replaced by something else entirely (see inner speech under new management)

Praying for your neighbor: - Gen 20:7, 17 — "he is a prophet, and he will pray for you" — God defines the prophetic office to Abimelech as intercession; Gen 18:22–33 — Abraham haggling mercy for Sodom - 1 Sam 12:23 — Samuel, to a people who had just rejected him: "far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD by ceasing to pray for you." Withholding intercession from people who wronged you is classified as sin — against God - Ps 106:23; Jer 15:1 — Moses "stood in the breach"; Moses and Samuel as the archetypal advocates (Exod 32:11–14, 31–32 — "blot me out")

Praying for your enemy: - Matt 5:44 — "love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you" — the command is precisely venue-transfer: the persecutor generates the most damning data of all, and Jesus routes it into intercession - Ps 35 — remarkably, the same psalm that files the riv-transfer ("Contend, O LORD, with those who contend with me," v. 1) also testifies: "when they were sick... I prayed with head bowed on my chest... as though I grieved for my friend" (vv. 13–14). Handing God the case and interceding for the adversary are one posture, not two - Isa 53:12 — the Servant "makes intercession for the transgressors"; Luke 23:34 — "Father, forgive them" - Acts 7:60 — Stephen's dying motion: "Lord, do not hold this sin against them" — courtroom language (do not reckon it to their account), filed by the murdered witness on behalf of his murderers. And standing in the crowd, holding the coats: Saul. The answered form of Stephen's advocacy is the apostle Paul — the most spectacular case-dismissal in church history

The page in one line

Riv asks only one question: whose cause are you carrying, and to which bench? The unlicensed litigator files against his neighbor in the courts of the kitchen and the heart; the advocate carries the same file to the throne of grace and pleads for the defendant — which is what the strong Redeemer has been doing with your file, day and night, all along (Mic 7:9; Heb 7:25).
