The Speck and the Log — Matthew 7:1–5

Part of Litigating Without a License. Jesus' own ruling on the unlicensed practice: the judging instrument is compromised, the judge's standard becomes his own sentence, and the goal — this is the surprise — is still to get the speck out.

The text

"Judge not, that you be not judged. For with the judgment you pronounce you will be judged, and with the measure you use it will be measured to you. Why do you see the speck (karphos) that is in your brother's eye, but do not notice the log (dokos) that is in your own eye? ... You hypocrite, first take the log out of your own eye, and then you will see clearly to take the speck out of your brother's eye." (Matt 7:1–5; parallel Luke 6:37–42, with the blind guides and the "good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over")

What "judge not" does and does not ban

It cannot be a ban on the judging faculty — the same chapter requires it: v. 6 (discern dogs and pigs), vv. 15–20 ("you will recognize them by their fruits"), and John 7:24 commands "judge with right judgment." The wiki's whole grammar applies: Din is a licensed faculty; what Jesus prohibits is the unlicensed practice — verdict-rendering on a brother from the bench of the silent tribunal, by a judge with an occluded eye, under a standard he could not himself survive. Krinō here is the condemning verdict, the sentence passed on a person — the accuser's act (see Diabolos — The Accuser) — not the discernment of fruit or the frank, face-to-face process of Lev 19:17 and Matt 18:15.

The measure: your standard becomes your sentence

"With the measure you use it will be measured to you." The unlicensed judge does not merely wrong his brother; he files his own sentencing guidelines. Every verdict you render publishes the standard you have agreed to stand under:

  • Rom 2:1–3 — "in passing judgment on another you condemn yourself, because you, the judge, practice the very same things" — the tribunal's rulings are self-incriminating testimony (cf. slander as autobiography)
  • Matt 18:23–35 — the unforgiving servant: the measure-principle as a full courtroom drama. Forgiven ten thousand talents, he seizes a man by the throat over a hundred denarii — and his master reinstates the original debt on the servant's own displayed standard: "should not you have had mercy... as I had mercy on you?"
  • James 2:13 — "judgment is without mercy to one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment"; Matt 5:7; 6:14–15 — the merciful obtain mercy
  • The measure cuts the other way too (Luke 6:38): "good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over" — generosity in judgment compounds like the severity does

Choose your measure the way a man chooses the terms of his own trial — because that is what it is.

The optics: what the log is

The log is not in the hand or the record — it is in the eye. Jesus locates the critic's sin in the judging instrument itself. Three consequences:

  1. Same material. Karphos and dokos are both wood — a splinter and a rafter of one substance. The critic's sin and the criticized sin differ in scale, not kind (Rom 2:1 again: "the very same things"). The tribunal always tries a version of its own case
  2. Distorted instrument. A log in the eye doesn't just add a blind spot; it bends everything seen around it. This is the epistemology of pride from Slander, Gossip, and the Busybody rendered as physiology: the judge is confident of his data and his interpretation precisely because his instrument is compromised — "how can you say to your brother, 'Let me take the speck out of your eye,' when there is the log in your own eye?" The offer sounds generous; the surgeon is blind. Luke's frame makes it lethal: "can a blind man lead a blind man?" (Luke 6:39)
  3. Disqualified witness, not abolished court. "Hypocrite" (hypokritēs) is stage language — playing the oculist while blind. The remedy is not to dismiss the case but to recuse and be treated first: "first take the log out of your own eye" — 1 Cor 11:31 ("if we judged ourselves truly, we should not be judged"), the one court where the believer holds a standing license: his own conduct (Rom 14:13 — "decide (krinate) never to put a stumbling block...")

"Then you will see clearly": the speck still comes out

The verse everyone forgets. Jesus does not say the speck is none of your business — a speck in the eye is a real injury to your brother, and leaving it there is not love. The endpoint of the whole saying is restorative eye surgery: "then you will see clearly to take the speck out of your brother's eye."

  • The sequence is the license: log-removal (repentance, self-judgment) is what qualifies the surgeon. Criticism after the log's removal doesn't get harsher — it gets cleaner, gentler, and rarer, because the man who has just had a rafter pulled from his own eye handles other people's corneas differently
  • Gal 6:1 — the same surgery described: restore "in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself" — the log-check built into the procedure
  • Matt 18:15; Lev 19:17; Prov 25:8–10 — the licensed venue: face to face, alone, confidential (Prov 25:8–10 developed at Riv)
  • And the deepest qualification: the only man with no log — "which one of you convicts me of sin?" (John 8:46) — is the one who said "neither do I condemn you" (John 8:11). Perfect eyesight chose advocacy. That settles what clear vision is for

From the Hall

"You ever have this where you're driving... somebody next to you is really pointing at something — what are you doing? — and then somebody else is pointing at you... And you drive off and you think, terrible drivers everywhere, just mind your own business. And then you pull in and you realize your trunk was open the whole time and stuff was flying out... And you go through with a self-righteous indignation — these are terrible drivers, what's gone wrong with people — until you finally park and you realize: oh, I had the problem. Everyone was trying to tell me something." — Kevin DeYoung, When Jesus Speaks Against You

The page in one line

Matthew 7 is the trial procedure of the kingdom: no verdict from a compromised instrument, no standard you can't stand under, self-judgment as the only standing license — and clear eyes given not for prosecution but for surgery.
