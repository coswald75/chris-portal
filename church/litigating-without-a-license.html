Litigating Without a License

A wiki on the critical spirit: contention, accusation, and their downstream sins — and the gospel that adjourns the courtroom of the heart.

Thesis

The capacity to weigh, charge, and plead — the din faculty — is a real gift, licensed to God, to the king, and to the advocate of the poor (Prov 31:8–9). Satan is the original unlicensed litigator: the diabolos, the katēgoros, prosecuting the brethren day and night. At the cross his office was abolished (Rev 12:10–11; Col 2:14–15; Rom 8:33–34) — the position was eliminated, the docket closed. The critical spirit is that decommissioned prosecutor's caseload, carried on by volunteers — in the silent courtroom of the heart, in the busybody's stolen jurisdiction, the whisper, and the slander that takes the devil's own name (mē diabolous, 1 Tim 3:11). The gospel's answer is not a better defense but a closed court: "It is God who justifies." And in that courtroom only two speaking roles remain — you are either an accuser or an advocate.

The wiki speaks a controlled vocabulary — see the Lexicon for every anchor phrase and its home page. For the whole project in two columns, see the master table: Madon vs Shalom. For the teaching sequence — course, tests, journaling, and book order — see the Course Map (in the Course subfolder).

Foundational layers

  1. Diabolos — The Accuser — the devil's core attribute: accuser of the brethren. Word study (diabolos, katēgoros, satan), the office and its abolition, quotes from the Hall (Watson, Carson, Boice, MacArthur, DeYoung, Alcantar, Oswald)
  2. Madon — the Hebrew anatomy of contention: madon/midyanim from the root din (litigation). Full occurrence survey, courtroom uses, and where the root turns positive
  3. The Internal Courtroom — the Silent Tribunal: contention before it is audible. Simon (Luke 7), the praying Pharisee (Luke 18), Michal (2 Sam 6), the demonic pedigree (James 3:14–16), Jesus hearing silent courts, the gospel adjournment — and inner speech under new management (Sarah vs. Michal; the Psalms' self-address, Ps 42:5; 103:1–2; 2 Cor 10:5)
  4. Din — the root redeemed: din runs downhill (Prov 31:8–9; Jer 22:16), the Servant's vindication behind Rom 8:33 (Isa 50:8–9), executing righteous judgment over the sinful-but-saved, and the accuser-or-advocate fork
  5. Slander, Gossip, and the Busybody — the downstream sins in one page: Greek/Hebrew word studies; slander defined as a verdict defensible with the data but not the truth (Simon, Rom 3:8, the dibbah of Num 13); the epistemology of pride; gossip as the fuel of madon; the busybody as discovery-without-jurisdiction
  6. Riv — the lawsuit word and prayer: God pleads the causes of his people (Prov 22:23; Mic 7:9); intercession as the licensed form of having a case about someone — brother, husband, neighbor, enemy (1 John 5:16; Luke 22:31–32; Acts 7:60)
  7. The Speck and the Log — Matthew 7:1–5: the measure you use as your own sentencing guideline, the log as the compromised judging instrument, and clear eyes given for surgery, not prosecution
  8. Accusing God — God in the Dock: the second direction of accusation, from Eden's indictment through the Numbers dibbah and Malachi's counter-suits to worry, anxiety, and fear as the quiet paperwork of a suit against heaven; lament as the licensed complaint; the dock accepted at the cross
  9. The Advocate (Paraklētos) — the facing page to Diabolos: called alongside to call out. Two Advocates covering two courtrooms (1 John 2:1; John 14:16; Rom 8:26 + 8:34); the Spirit's threefold sight — the saint as he was, as he is, as he will be (advocacy from the decree, not wishful thinking); Lewis's "no ordinary people"; Barnabas as the human case study; encouragement and affirmation as deputized advocacy (Heb 3:13; Crabtree, Practicing Affirmation)
  10. Eshet Midyanim — the women page, both directions: eshet midyanim vs. eshet chayil (Prov 12:4 holds both). The fear-or-hope engine (1 Pet 3:5–6); the Pauline feminine expressions (1 Tim 5:11–15 — the gossip/busybody circuit, "strayed after Satan"; Titus 2:3–5 — the circuit redeemed into discipleship); the accusing gallery (Job's wife, Michal, Jezebel, Potiphar's wife, Zeresh, Herodias); the advocating gallery (Abigail the flagship, Esther, the persistent widow vs. the antidikos, Tekoa, Deborah, Hannah, Ruth, Priscilla, Prov 31); the office qualification (1 Tim 3:11 mē diabolous; Titus 2:3)

  11. Ish Midyanim — the men page: charcoal to embers (Prov 26:21). The engine of threatened glory — pride and envy prosecuting rivals (Prov 13:10; 1 Sam 18:9; Matt 27:18; 1 Tim 6:4–5's anatomy ending in "evil suspicions"); the accusing gallery (Cain, Korah, Saul, Doeg, Shimei, Sanballat, the elder brother, Diotrephes, Daniel's accusers); the advocating gallery (Jonathan the narrative flagship, Paul-for-Onesimus the epistolary flagship, Moses, Joseph, Boaz, the dissenting councilmen); the male office qualifications (amachos, 1 Tim 3:3; Titus 1:7; 2 Tim 2:24–26; 1 Tim 2:8)

  12. Shalom — the terminal word: what stands where madon stood. The shillem root (peace as the settled account); righteousness first, then peace (Ps 85:10; Heb 7:2; Rom 5:1); the God of peace crushing Satan (Rom 16:20); peace as garrison (Phil 4:7) and umpire (Col 3:15); peacemaking vs. peace-mongering (Jer 6:14); and joy as the evidence of shalom (Ps 4:7–8; James 1:2; 1 Pet 1:6–8; Heb 13:17)

  13. Ingratitude — rigging the record: evidence-suppression as the discovery fraud behind every suit against providence (Rom 1:21 as the indictment's first count; forgetting as re-attribution, Deut 8:17; the ten lepers' audit rate); Naomi's "empty" with Ruth standing right there — and the narrative refutation (Ruth 4:15); the Narcan for the Soul diagnosis (discipline of the Lord; hell as the false audit granted); thanksgiving as evidence-restoration, with the Eucharist as institutionalized exhibit-preservation
  14. Babel Undone — the redemptive-history page: Pentecost as the corporate arrival of shalom. Babel as madon's linguistic monument reversed (via Zeph 3:9) by the Paraclete's first public miracle — a common tongue whose vocabulary is the gospel of peace; three thousand dockets closed in a day; and Deuteronomy's forfeited festival finally kept (Deut 28:47 → Acts 2:46, "glad and generous hearts")
  15. Madon vs Shalom — the master table: the whole project in two columns, twenty-eight rows from etymology to terminus; site map, diagnostic, and sermon skeleton
  16. Lexicon — the controlled vocabulary: every anchor phrase, its one-line definition, and its home page, plus the editorial rules for future pages

Application pages

  • Madon in Marriage — Gottman's Four Horsemen as courtroom conduct: criticism = the prosecution (complaint vs. criticism = data vs. verdict), contempt = the verdict from the bench (Michal; "rottenness in his bones" = the immune-system finding), defensiveness = Adam's counter-suit, stonewalling = fleeing a court still in session; the antidotes translated (soft startup = the licensed venue; fondness-and-admiration = evidences of grace); the engines and the closed docket as what the lab cannot supply
  • Madon and the Anxious Tone-Setter — Friedman's failure-of-nerve framework theologized: the chronically anxious member as a litigant with a standing suit against providence; the parable of the talents as the audit (burial = accusation with a shovel); Kadesh-barnea as the case study (ten anxious tone-setters capture a nation overnight; God reads the anxiety as despising him, Num 14:11); Aaron and the gospel of niceness; self-differentiation as theology ("I don't work for you — I work for the Lord"); the glued domino — the well-differentiated leader as advocate of God and man, with faith's open aperture (2 Kgs 6:17; Heb 11:6; 2 Cor 5:16) and madon replaced by shalom; minister to anxiety, never organize around it
  • Madon and Mimesis — Girard theologized: borrowed desire as madon's supply chain (Eccl 4:4; James 4:1–3); the scapegoat mechanism ending at the accuser's front door (Girard's own Satan-vs-Paraclete conclusion); zero-sum theology — covetousness as objection to God's provisioning, envy as objection to God's allocation (Malthus vs. Gilder; Luke 15:31; John 3:26–30); comparison as the thief of joy — "a king like the nations" (1 Sam 8 → 1 Kgs 12); the only safe Model (Phil 2:5–6; 1 Cor 11:1)
  • Madon and Radical Egalitarianism — disputing God's providential disbursement of power: Eden's leveling pitch and Satan's counterfeit kingdom; the disbursement doctrine (Ps 75:6–7; Dan 4:17; John 19:11); ontological equality + complementarity of station (church, government, family); the WLC on the fifth commandment (Q124–132 — "grieving at the advancement of another" as sin); the gallery of levelers (Miriam, Korah, Dathan, Absalom's kiss, the disciples, John 12:19); the critical-theory subsection (the hermeneutic of accusation; society-as-madon; the courtroom with no closed docket); the true order (Magnificat, 1 Pet 5:6, the towel at the table)
