Ingratitude — Rigging the Record

Part of Litigating Without a License. If slander is a verdict defensible with the data but not the truth, ingratitude is how the data gets rigged. It is evidence-suppression — the deletion of exhibits from God's file — and it is therefore the discovery-phase fraud behind every suit against providence: the case against God only ever looks defensible after the benefits have been forgotten. Ingratitude is not a mood; it is tampering with the record.

The word, and its rank in the indictment

  • Rom 1:21 — the first count in Paul's indictment of the human race, upstream of idolatry itself: "although they knew God, they did not honor him as God or give thanks (ēucharistēsan), and they became futile in their thinking, and their foolish hearts were darkened." Note the sequence: the withheld thanksgiving comes first; the darkened mind is its consequence. Ingratitude is not one sin among many — it is the fall's opening move, the original deletion of exhibits
  • 2 Tim 3:2 — acharistoi, "ungrateful," in the last-days vice list — two entries from diaboloi (slanderers). The lexicon knows the family resemblance: the unthankful mouth and the slandering mouth are neighbors
  • Luke 6:35 — and the counter-fact about God: "he is kind to the ungrateful (acharistous) and the evil" — the Father goes on filing kindnesses into records that keep deleting them

The mechanics: deleted exhibits

Scripture's word for the mechanism is forgetting — and it treats forgetting not as a memory failure but as a moral act:

  • Ps 103:2 — "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits" — the commanded self-address (inner speech under new management) exists precisely because the benefits will be deleted unless recited
  • Ps 106:13, 21 — "they soon forgot his works... They forgot God, their Savior, who had done great things in Egypt" — the wilderness generation's grumbling docket was built on a purged evidence file: the Red Sea was weeks old when "has the LORD brought us out to kill us?" got filed
  • Deut 8:11–17 — and forgetting's endgame: "lest... you forget the LORD your God... and you say in your heart, 'My power and the might of my hand have gotten me this wealth.'" Deleted exhibits don't leave the record blank — they get re-attributed. Ingratitude erases the Giver's name from the gifts and writes its own over the top
  • Num 11:6 — the manna contempt: "there is nothing at all but this manna" — daily supernatural provision, entered into the record as nothing at all. That is the audit style of the ungrateful heart: the standing miracle becomes line-item zero
  • Luke 17:15–18 — the audit's usual return rate: "Were not ten cleansed? Where are the nine?" One leper in ten files the exhibit; Jesus asks the question out loud so the ratio goes on the record

Case study: Naomi at the gate of Bethlehem

Ruth 1 stages ingratitude with the deleted exhibit standing in the frame. Naomi has just received one of the most magnificent pledges of covenant loyalty in all of Scripture — "where you go I will go... your people shall be my people, and your God my God; where you die I will die" (1:16–17) — from a daughter-in-law who abandoned her country, her family, and her prospects to walk beside her. And then the two women arrive at Bethlehem, the town stirs, the women say "Is this Naomi?" — and Naomi, with Ruth standing right there, answers:

"Do not call me Naomi; call me Mara, for the Almighty has dealt very bitterly with me. I went away full, and the LORD has brought me back empty." (1:20–21)

Empty. The word is an audit — and it is false with the evidence physically present. The mis-audit slanders two parties in one sentence: it indicts God (the Almighty has dealt bitterly; he brought me back empty — a dibbah on providence, filed at the city gate) and it discounts Ruth — the pledge just received is entered into the record at zero, the hesed standing at her elbow deleted like manna ("nothing at all"). Ingratitude always does this double damage: every suppressed exhibit has both a Giver and, usually, a human courier, and both are slandered by the deletion.

Two pastoral notes keep the reading honest. First, Naomi is lamenting, and her grief is real — dead husband, dead sons, actual famine-scarred years; the book never has God rebuke her, and lament, not mutiny applies: she is at least saying it at Bethlehem, headed the right direction. But lament that crosses into a false audit gets answered — not with rebuke, but with narrative refutation: the rest of the book is God quietly re-entering the deleted exhibit into evidence, until the same women of Bethlehem who heard "call me Mara" hand down the corrected ruling over the same woman: "your daughter-in-law who loves you, who is more to you than seven sons, has given birth to him" (4:15) — and Naomi ends the book with Obed on her lap, the "empty" verdict overturned in open court by the gift she had discounted. Second: the exhibit she deleted was carrying the Messiah's line. The gift ingratitude rates at zero is routinely the one God is doing the most through.

From the pulpit: the Narcan diagnosis

The recent Ecclesiastes sermon Narcan for the Soul supplies this page's physiology — Ecclesiastes as "spiritual Narcan," displacing "fantasy with reality and romanticism with accounting" (hebel and yitron — vapor and net):

"Do you know anybody for whom enjoyment itself is just constantly elusive? Their brain seems to be wired for ingratitude. They simply can't be grateful for the things the way that they are. And it's just a constant drip of discontentment, no matter what their circumstances." — Narcan for the Soul

Mark the phrase: a constant drip — Proverbs' own image for the eshet midyanim (19:13; 27:15). Ingratitude is contention's posture aimed upward: the ungrateful heart is a contentious spouse toward God, the continual dripping in his house. And the sermon presses to the two hard edges:

"You will not be able to live the life that God's called you to live unless he gives you the ability to enjoy what he's given you... Your lack of contentment — at least you should strongly consider that it's discipline from the Lord... the consequence of a consistent state of ingratitude that you have been warned about, and a constant taking God and his gifts for granted." — Narcan for the Soul (on Eccl 2:24–25; 3:13 — enjoyment itself is a gift, withdrawable; cf. Heb 12:5–11)

"Hell is the absolute negation of every gift of God. It's functionally the place where ungrateful people experience the reality that they were always believing in — that God isn't blessing them, that God isn't caring for them." — Narcan for the Soul

That last line is Rom 1:21 carried to term, and it belongs beside the one-talent servant (the hard-master verdict): judgment as the false audit granted. The ungrateful record — "no benefits on file" — becomes, at last, the sentence: a world with no benefits on file. The rigged ledger is the one thing God will eventually let you keep.

The remedy: thanksgiving as the un-rigging of the record

Thanksgiving is not sentiment; it is evidence-restoration — the commanded verdict that re-enters the exhibits, exactly parallel to joy's "count it all joy":

  • 1 Thess 5:18 — "give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you" — all circumstances, because the exhibits are present in all circumstances; Eph 5:20 — "always and for everything"
  • Phil 4:6 — the anxiety protocol runs on it: "in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving" — petition without thanksgiving is just the suit against providence re-filed politely; the thanksgiving clause is what makes the request an act of trust rather than a complaint with an address
  • Col 3:15–17 — the settled-accounts cluster: peace umpiring, "and be thankful... with thankfulness in your hearts... giving thanks to God the Father" — three times in three verses; shalom and gratitude are installed together
  • Gratitude as epistemic defense: "To have boundless gratitude, to be overwhelmed with thanksgiving — it's a joyful guardian against false belief" (Chris Oswald, Centered in Christ, on Col 2:6–7) — the guarded record cannot be rigged; and as contentment's prescription: "You come to Him with a heart of thanksgiving and it will sow contentment into your heart" (same sermon)
  • Gratitude as humility's soil: "Thankfulness is a soil in which pride does not easily grow" (John Stott, quoted in C.J. Mahaney, The Perils of Pride) — because Deut 8's re-attribution ("my power, my hand") cannot survive a recited benefits list; and the warning side: "If that goodness is scorned, if we do not let it draw us to God in gratitude... we have only ourselves to blame" (J.I. Packer, quoted in Mahaney, The Inauguration of Grace, on Rom 2:4)
  • Gratitude toward people is the same discipline horizontally: Paul's letters open with thanksgiving for observed grace (evidences of grace) — the anti-Naomi move: entering the human couriers of God's hesed into the record by name (Rom 16!), and "we are forgetful people... when we don't take time to stop and remember what God has done, we find ourselves being ungrateful" (Chris Oswald, A Gatekeeper's Song)
  • And the church's central act is the record kept in bread and wine: Eucharist — "he took bread, and when he had given thanks..." (Luke 22:19, eucharistēsas) — "do this in remembrance of me." The Lord's Table is institutionalized exhibit-preservation: the chief evidence of God's goodness, re-entered weekly, so that no famine, diagnosis, or Mara-season can get it deleted. As Narcan for the Soul ends: the preacher of Ecclesiastes says eat and drink and enjoy your toil — "and what did God give us to remember the labor of Jesus for us? He gave us something to eat and drink"

The page in one line

Ingratitude is the rigged ledger — Rom 1:21's withheld thanks darkening the mind, Naomi's "empty" with the exhibit standing beside her, manna entered as nothing at all — and thanksgiving is discovery done honestly: the benefits recited, the couriers named, the chief exhibit kept on the table in bread and wine, until the audit says what the record always said: my cup overflows.
