Diabolos (διάβολος) — The Accuser

Part of Litigating Without a License. The critical spirit flows from one of the devil's core attributes: he is the diabolos, the katēgoros — the prosecuting attorney of the heavenly courtroom. Every unlicensed litigator is working his old caseload.

The words

Term Greek/Hebrew Meaning
diabolos διάβολος "Slanderer, accuser" — from διαβάλλω, to throw across: to hurl a charge. The LXX's standard rendering of Hebrew satan
katēgoros / katēgōr κατήγορος / κατήγωρ The courtroom prosecutor — the technical term for the accusing counsel (Rev 12:10; cf. Acts 23:30, 35; 24:8)
satan שָׂטָן "Adversary" — used with the article in Job 1–2 and Zech 3 (ha-satan, "the adversary/prosecutor"); as a verb, "to accuse, oppose at law" (Ps 109:6, "let an accuser stand at his right hand")

The devil's two names are two halves of one job: satan names the adversary's posture (against you), diabolos names his method (the thrown charge).

The scripture set

The office in session (pre-cross): - Job 1–2 — the adversary's opening briefs: accusing Job to God ("Does Job fear God for no reason?") — note that he accuses the motive when the record is clean - Zech 3:1–5 — Satan standing at Joshua's right hand (the accuser's legal position, Ps 109:6) to accuse; the evidence is real (filthy garments); the LORD rebukes the prosecutor and reclothes the defendant. Justification silences the accuser without disputing the facts - Ps 109:6 — "let an accuser (satan) stand at his right hand" — the standard courtroom staging

The office abolished (at the cross): - Rev 12:10–11 — "the accuser (katēgōr) of our brothers has been thrown down, who accuses them day and night before our God... they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" - Col 2:14–15 — the record of debt nailed to the cross; the rulers disarmed and "put to open shame" - Rom 8:33–34 — "Who shall bring any charge (enkalesei) against God's elect? It is God who justifies. Who is to condemn?" — the docket is closed - 1 John 2:1 — "we have an Advocate (paraklētos — defense counsel) with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous" - Heb 7:25; 10:21–22 — the standing Intercessor; the conscience sprinkled clean

The accuser's three directions (see quotes below): 1. He accuses us to God — Job 1–2; Zech 3; Rev 12:10 2. He accuses God to us — Gen 3:1–5 ("Did God actually say...?" — the first slander in Scripture is a slander of God's character); Watson: "He is a hard Master." Full development at Accusing God (worry, anxiety, and fear as the quiet paperwork of this suit) 3. He accuses us to one another and to ourselves — the conscience (Heb 10:22), and the church's quarrels (2 Cor 2:10–11 — unforgiveness as Satan's "design"; 1 Tim 3:11; Titus 2:3, where slanderous speech takes his very name, mē diabolous — see Slander, Gossip, and the Busybody)

The mouth that takes his name: - 1 Tim 3:11; Titus 2:3; 2 Tim 3:3 — humans described as diaboloi. To slander is to do the devil's work in his own name. The bridge text between this page and Madon: when the vertical office was abolished, the unlicensed practice moved into kitchens, pews, and hearts. See The Internal Courtroom

From the Hall

Citations by sermon title and date, as preserved in the corpus.

Thomas Watson — the Puritan statement of the double slander:

"Satan is implacably malicious, and as he sometimes accuses us to God—so he accuses God to us, and says, 'He is a hard Master and his commandments are grievous.'" — The Beatitudes — Appendix: His Commandments Are Not Grievous (on 1 John 5:3)

And the terror of the reverse — when even mercy turns witness:

"If mercy is an accuser, who shall be our advocate? The sinner never escapes hell, when mercy draws up the indictment." — Body of Divinity

D.A. Carson — how the defense actually answers:

"What does Satan do? He accuses them. He accuses them day and night. He'll work on your conscience and make you feel as dirty and as guilty and as defeated... as he possibly can. And what shall you say? 'Oh, I'm not that bad'? You'll never beat him that way. Never. No, what you say is: 'Satan, I'm even worse than you think. But God loves me anyway. He has accepted me because of the blood of the Lamb.'" — Rage, Rage, Against the Church

James Boice — Zechariah 3 as the justification picture:

"Satan, the accuser of the brethren, is certainly there to point it out. How can this man represent the people before God when he's so wicked himself? And what we have is a wonderful picture of justification, because God tells the angel to remove his dirty clothes and put a new robe upon him and a clean turban on his head." — Spiritual Warfare (2005-05-01)

John MacArthur — the defense counsel:

"He intercedes for us. He's the lawyer for our defense. He knows we're weak. He sympathizes with our weakness." — A Vision of Christ's Work in His Church (on Rom 8:31–34)

Kevin DeYoung — the gospel's answer to the accused conscience:

"You know all too well the devil's many accusations, and here comes, with great lightning and thunder, the power of God in the gospel... and pulls back the curtain, and the revelation of the gospel there is not your righteousness." — The Righteousness of God

Ricky Alcantar — the accusation as Satan's chief weapon:

"The big attack of Satan is accusation... What he's called here is the accuser of our brothers, and the thing that he had that was most deadly was his accusation." — Here There Be Dragons (2022-05-15)

"Brother, the way to silence the enemy is with the blood of the Lamb. That is the weapon laid out for us in Revelation chapter 12." — Here There Be Dragons (2022-05-15)

Chris Oswald — the office eliminated, and where the practice relocates:

"Satan's role as accuser is a factual role. He is merely bringing up what is actually true of you and me... Satan's main strategy with accusation is to present the reality of our sins before the Lord as a kind of prosecuting attorney in the heavenly courtroom, inciting God's judgment against us." — Spiritual Warfare and the Psalms, Part 2 (2025-06-22)

"The cross and resurrection of Jesus ripped Satan of this central strategy. He simply lost a job. Satan got DOGE'd — this position was eliminated at the cross." — Spiritual Warfare and the Psalms, Part 2 (2025-06-22)

"Satan has no standing any longer in God's throne room, God's courtroom. He may still have standing in your courtroom if your conscience, if your heart isn't fully standing on the gospel of Jesus Christ." — Spiritual Warfare and the Psalms, Part 2 (2025-06-22)

"He's lost accusatory power up there. He'll use accusatory power in here... Anybody, family, church, or society that is not convinced of the gospel is a body ready to be hijacked by satanic bitterness." — Spiritual Warfare and the Psalms, Part 2 (2025-06-22)

"Satan's best weapon is your unforgiven sin. Your sin armed Satan with a great arsenal of accusation... God took every bomb and bullet in Satan's accusatory arsenal for you." — Spiritual Warfare and the Psalms, Part 2 (2025-06-22)

"I've learned that God does not use the accuser of the brethren to edify and teach. If the only person telling you you have a problem in that area is some fork-tongued devil of an accuser, I'd probably just move on." — When a Snake Takes the Stand (2024-10-20)

The thesis connection

Put Watson and the Spiritual Warfare series together and the project's spine appears:

  1. The accuser held a real office with a real caseload — and the evidence was genuine (Zech 3: the garments were filthy; "Satan's role as accuser is a factual role")
  2. The cross abolished the office — not by disputing the evidence but by exhausting the sentence (Col 2:14; Rev 12:10–11). The position was eliminated
  3. Therefore every act of accusation since is litigating without a license — a decommissioned prosecutor's work carried on by volunteers. When a believer runs the internal courtroom, presses perpetual madon, oversees another's household, or leaks verdicts — they are taking up a caseload God closed, in a court whose docket is empty (Rom 8:33–34)
  4. The only litigation still licensed is the Advocate's — and the din of the defenseless (Prov 31:8–9)
