Din (דִּין) — The Root Redeemed

Part of Litigating Without a License. Madon is this root gone sour. Din itself — the faculty of weighing, charging, pleading, ruling — is good, and licensed, whenever the right judge holds the court. The redeemed use of the contentious faculty is not silence; it is advocacy: executing the true court's verdict over God's people.

The word

Din (verb דִּין, noun דִּין): to judge, govern, plead a cause, vindicate; a lawsuit, a legal claim, justice. The name Dan ("God has judged/vindicated me," Gen 30:6) and Daniel ("God is my judge") carry it. Its degenerate child is madon — litigation without license. The question the whole wiki turns on: who may hold this word?

Din in the right hands

God the Judge — and note what his din does for his people: - Deut 32:36 (= Ps 135:14) — "the LORD will vindicate (yadin) his people and have compassion on his servants, when he sees that their power is gone." Din here is not threat but rescue — judgment rendered for the powerless. (Quoted at Heb 10:30) - Gen 30:6 — "God has judged me (dananni)... and given me a son" — Rachel names vindication - Ps 54:1 — "O God... vindicate me (tedineni) by your might" - Ps 140:12 — "the LORD maintains the cause (din) of the afflicted, and will execute justice for the needy" - 1 Sam 2:10; Ps 7:8; 9:8; 96:10; Gen 15:14 — the Judge of all the earth

The king: - Ps 72:1–2 — "he will judge (yadin) your people with righteousness, and your poor with justice"; Jer 21:12

The advocate of the voiceless: - Prov 31:8–9 — "Open your mouth for the mute... judge righteously (din), defend the rights (din) of the poor and needy." Same root as madon, used twice, positively — and in the same chapter as the excellent wife. Lemuel's mother teaches the redeemed use of the contentious faculty: open your mouth for someone - Jer 22:16 — "He judged the cause (dan din) of the poor and needy; then it was well. Is not this to know me? declares the LORD." Licensed din is not merely permitted — it is knowledge of God - Jer 5:28 — the negative print: "they judge not with justice the cause (din) of the fatherless... and they do not defend the rights of the needy"

The pattern is unmistakable: everywhere din is wielded lawfully, it runs downhill — from the strong to the defenseless, for them. Madon is din running sideways and upward: neighbor against neighbor, creature against Judge.

Christ under din — and the source of Romans 8:33

The deepest redemption of the root: the Judge submitted to the court.

  • Isa 50:8–9 — the Servant: "He who vindicates me is near. Who will contend with me? Let us stand up together. Who is my adversary? Let him come near to me. Behold, the Lord GOD helps me; who will declare me guilty?" — This is the text behind Rom 8:33–34. Paul puts the Servant's own courtroom confidence into the mouth of everyone in Christ
  • 1 Tim 3:16 — "he was manifested in the flesh, vindicated (edikaiōthē) by the Spirit" — the resurrection as verdict
  • Rom 4:25 — "raised for our justification" — his vindication is the elect's vindication
  • (John Stott, quoted in the Hall: "He who had been condemned for us in his death was publicly vindicated in his resurrection" — Death Swallowed Up in Victory, C.J. Mahaney)

Executing righteous judgment over the sinful-but-saved

Here is the practice this page exists for. When you look at a believer and see real sin — remember, the accuser's evidence was real too (Zech 3: the garments were filthy) — righteous din is announcing the true court's ruling over them, not reopening the case:

  • Rom 8:33–34 — "Who shall bring any charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies." The docket is closed. To proclaim Christ's righteousness over a struggling saint is not sentimentality; it is accurate courtroom reporting
  • Rom 14:4 — "Who are you to pass judgment on the servant of another? It is before his own master that he stands or falls. And he will be upheld, for the Lord is able to make him stand." — The verse doesn't just forbid the charge; it hands you the announcement: say the second sentence over them
  • 2 Cor 5:16–21 — "we regard no one according to the flesh... entrusting to us the message of reconciliation. Therefore, we are ambassadors for Christ" — an ambassador is a herald of a verdict already rendered elsewhere. That is the believer's licensed speech-role toward the justified
  • Zech 3:4–5 — be the voice in the vision: "Remove the filthy garments from him... I will clothe you with pure vestments." And note the prophet joining in — "let them put a clean turban on his head" — intercession as participation in the reclothing
  • John 8:10–11 — "Neither do I condemn you; go, and sin no more" — vindication first, then the charge to holiness; the order is the gospel
  • Rom 15:7; Col 3:12–13 — welcome and forgive as the Lord has — your treatment of the saint executes the court's ruling
  • Job 42:7–8 — the case study in reverse: the friends prosecuted a man God had justified ("you have not spoken of me what is right") — and the accused becomes their advocate: "my servant Job shall pray for you"
  • Prov 10:12 — the hinge verse, containing both sides of the fork in one line: "Hatred stirs up contentions (medanim), but love covers all offenses" (cf. 1 Pet 4:8; James 5:19–20 — restoration as covering)

A guardrail: advocacy is not flattery, and covering is not concealing. Licensed din still speaks frankly about sin — but face to face, for restoration: Lev 19:17 ("reason frankly with your neighbor"), Matt 18:15, Gal 6:1, 2 Tim 4:2. The advocate tells the whole truth under the verdict, the way the Advocate does (1 John 1:9–2:1 — confession and advocacy in one breath). What the advocate never does is re-file the closed charge — against the brother, or in the silent tribunal.

Accuser or advocate: the only two roles left

In the courtroom of Revelation 12 there are exactly two speaking parts remaining, and every believer's mouth — and heart — is cast in one of them:

  • The accuser — katēgoros, disbarred, still soliciting volunteers (see Diabolos — The Accuser)
  • The Advocate — paraklētos (1 John 2:1), whose junior counsel we are: "they have conquered him by the blood of the Lamb and by the word of their testimony" (Rev 12:11) — testimony (martyria) is courtroom speech; the saints overcome the prosecutor by taking the stand for the defense

"Truly humbled souls... would not dare to approach the throne of the God of holiness if it were not for him, our Lord, our Advocate, who can abide in the heavenly temple, because his righteousness endures forever." — Spurgeon, Treasury of David — Psalm 15

"He was and still is a prevailing Pleader. Our Advocate on high returns not empty from the throne of grace." — Spurgeon, Treasury of David — Psalm 21

"Let us also be glad, because the Judge of all the earth will vindicate our righteous cause." — Spurgeon, Treasury of David — Psalm 16

There is no licensed third role. Neutrality about a justified saint's standing is not available: to withhold the announcement of vindication from a brother drowning in accusation is to leave the accuser's version as the only voice in his courtroom. You are either an accuser or an advocate.

The page in one line

Din runs downhill or it curdles: judgment wielded for the weak is the knowledge of God (Jer 22:16); judgment wielded against the justified is Madon wearing a robe — and the only two vocations left in the courtroom are the disbarred prosecutor's and the prevailing Pleader's.
